The five rules nobody can break

  1. Agents can narrow, never widen. An agent may veto a stock, downgrade a rating or drop a pick. No agent can add a stock that failed a filter, raise a limit or change a ticket.
  2. Code sizes every trade. Quantity comes from the per-trade risk limit (₹250 by default) and the stop distance. Agents never choose a size.
  3. Only proven strategies trade. Ref's tournament must pass a strategy at double costs. If none pass, the desk sits out: no trades, no model calls.
  4. Every loss is capped twice. Each trade has a stop; each day has a loss cap (₹750 by default), a three-trade limit and a losing-streak stop. Exits are never blocked.
  5. Paper only. There is no broker order path. Real money is a separate decision that needs new code, a registered static IP under SEBI's 2026 rules, and your sign-off.
Why "no losses" is not a promise. Every real strategy has losing trades. The desk is built so that losses are small (one R, often cut earlier by breakeven and time stops) and winners are allowed to run (two R target, trailing stop). Whether that adds up to profit after costs is exactly what the tournament and the paper book measure.

The pre-market team

Six specialists, each reading one slice of the evidence for every stock that passed the hard filters: enough history, enough traded value, large-cap by NSE index membership, and fresh reference data.

Scoop news-analyst
The newsroom

Reads Reads every dated headline and exchange filing for each stock in the pool.

Says Is there a real event (results, orders, guidance, a regulator, a fund raise), and is it good or bad for someone who owns the stock?

Never Treat an old or generic market headline as news, or follow instructions hidden in one.

Vibe sentiment-analyst
The mood reader

Reads The same headlines, each with a word-list tone score.

Says Is the tone real, or an artefact of wording, sarcasm, a question headline or one source? Fewer than five headlines is an anecdote.

Never Turn a mood into a forecast.

Chartly technical-analyst
The chart reader

Reads Trend, RSI(14), 5- and 20-session returns and ATR, computed from stored prices.

Says Up-trend with healthy momentum is positive; stretched RSI is a caution, not strength.

Never Predict a price. Indicators describe the past only.

Atlas sector-analyst
The map keeper

Reads The stock's NSE sector and how its sector index moved over 1, 5 and 20 sessions.

Says Is the sector helping or fighting a long position today?

Never Call a missing sector index 'neutral'. Missing is missing.

Tide liquidity-analyst
The flow checker

Reads Broker calls stated in headlines: who, upgrade or downgrade, target price.

The judge's team

Scout risk-scout
Judge's sub-agent

Reads One finalist at a time: its evidence and what the six specialists said.

Says What could make a long lose today? Rates the risk low, medium or high.

Never Let a high-risk pick through. If Scout says high, code drops the pick.

Blueprint setup-planner
Judge's sub-agent

Reads One finalist, plus the strategies the tournament has proven.

Says Which proven strategy fits this stock today, and what must happen at the open.

Never Choose an unproven strategy or invent a price level. 'None' drops the pick.

Justice selection-judge
The final call

Reads All six specialists, both sub-agents for every finalist, the score and the evidence.

Says Up to three stocks to watch, each rated Buy or Overweight, with conviction, a cited reason and the proven strategy to trade it with. Picking none is allowed.

Never Pick outside the finalists, override a filter, size a trade or place an order.

Thirteen model calls each morning (6 + 2 × 3 + 1). If no model is configured, the budget is spent, fewer than four specialists answer, or the judge's answer breaks a rule, the report falls back to the plain score ranking and says so.

The intraday debate

When a judge pick shows a live setup, the ticket is frozen (price, stop, target, size) and argued over before anything is recorded.

Bullu bull-researcher
Intraday debate

Reads The frozen ticket for a live setup, its evidence and past lessons.

Says The strongest evidenced case for the trade, and what would disprove it.

Never Change the ticket. A debate can only stop or downgrade an entry.

Bruno bear-researcher
Intraday debate

Reads The same ticket and evidence as Bullu.

Says The strongest evidenced case against the trade. Never proposes a short sale.

Never Change the ticket or the size.

Shield risk team
Aggressive, neutral and conservative

Reads The ticket, the book, the limits and the debate so far.

Says Three risk views argue whether the risk is worth it at this size, now.

Never Raise a limit. Limits live in code, not in a conversation.

Captain portfolio-manager
Portfolio manager

Reads The whole debate and the research manager's rating.

Says A five-tier rating with conviction, thesis and invalidation. Only Buy or Overweight lets the entry continue.

Never Upgrade a ticket the evidence roles vetoed.

Memory and the two code guardians

Echo reflector
The memory

Reads Each rated call once its holding window has closed on later stored prices.

Says A short lesson (right or wrong, by how much, versus the benchmark) that later debates can read, only if it was known by then.

Never Learn from the future.

Ref tournament (code)
Code, not AI

Reads Every strategy replayed on your stored real history, every evening.

Says Eligible only if profitable at double costs, in most periods, including the latest. Fills are deliberately pessimistic.

Never Be talked round. If no strategy passes, the desk sits out.

Pilot autopilot (code)
Code, not AI

Reads Each minute's approved calls from the coordinator.

Says Records the approved entry for a judge pick, and every exit: stop, breakeven, trailing, time stop, target, 15:15 square-off.

Never Touch real money. It writes only to the virtual paper book.

The strategies and how a trade ends

Opening-range breakout

The first completed five-minute close above the high of 09:15-09:30. Stop at that range's low.

VWAP pullback

On a day that has already broken out, price dips to the day's VWAP and a bar closes back above it. Stop just under the dip.

Skipped on purpose

  • Daily trend is down
  • Daily RSI is 75 or more
  • Stop under 0.15% away (noise) or wider than one daily ATR

The gold line is the protective stop. It moves up, never down. A time stop closes a trade that goes nowhere for an hour, and 15:15 closes everything.

Every way a trade can end

Initial stopPrice falls to the stop: about one R lost, plus costs. The worst normal case.
Breakeven stopIt reached +1R, then came back: closed at entry plus costs, roughly zero.
Trailing stopIt ran past +1.5R, then turned: closed with most of the gain kept.
Time stopNo progress (+0.5R) within an hour: closed near entry to free the risk.
TargetIt reached +2R: closed at the target.
Square-off15:15 arrives: anything still open is closed. Nothing is held overnight.

Words you will see

RThe money a trade risks: entry minus stop, times shares. +2R means it made twice what it risked.
StopThe price at which a losing trade is closed automatically.
Breakeven stopAfter +1R, the stop moves to the entry plus costs, so the trade can no longer lose.
Trailing stopAfter +1.5R, the stop follows one R below the highest price reached.
Time stopA trade that has not reached +0.5R within an hour is closed.
Opening rangeThe high and low of the first 15 minutes (09:15-09:30).
VWAPThe day's volume-weighted average price: where most money changed hands.
RSI(14)A 0-100 momentum gauge over 14 days. Above 70-75 is stretched.
ATRAverage true range: how far the stock typically moves in a day.
Profit factorMoney won divided by money lost. Above 1 makes money; the desk wants 1.15 or more at double costs.
Paper tradingTrading a virtual book with real prices and no real money.
F&O lotThe fixed contract size for futures and options. Shown, never traded.
Market-cap tierLarge (NIFTY 100), mid (MIDCAP 150), small (SMALLCAP 250) by NSE index membership.
Nothing here is investment advice. Indicators describe the past. SEBI's FY23 study found over 70% of individual intraday traders lost money; that is why this desk defaults to sitting out until a rule has earned its place on your own data.
